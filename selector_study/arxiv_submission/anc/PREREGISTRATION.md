# Pre-registration: solver selection from a cheap spectral probe

**Locked before any solve was run.** Numberz.ai Inc. Written 13 September 2026.

Everything below is fixed in advance. Any change after the first result is read is recorded as a
dated addendum beneath this line and never as an edit above it, so a changed hash is the intended
signal.

## 0. Scope and what this study is not

This study uses only classical, published preconditioners and a probe we implement ourselves. It
contains no learned solver, no neural model, and no material from any collaborator. It is not a
replication of anyone else's result.

## 1. Operator families

Six families. Every operator is generated by the accompanying script from a declared
discretisation, so the study reproduces with no data download. Each family carries a swept
parameter, because the case of interest is an operator that moves while the problem stays the same.

| ID | Family | Swept parameter |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | 2D heterogeneous conduction, 5-point | log-normal conductivity contrast |
| F2 | 3D conduction, 7-point | contrast |
| F3 | 2D anisotropic diffusion | anisotropy ratio |
| F4 | 2D conduction with a jump coefficient on inclusions | jump magnitude |
| F5 | Graph Laplacian of a random geometric graph, shifted to SPD | shift sigma |
| F6 | Shifted stiffness, K + cM, from a 2D mass and stiffness pair | shift c |

Sizes are drawn from a declared list and are the same for every method on a given operator.
Right-hand side is a fixed pseudo-random vector per operator, seeded from the operator id.

## 2. Candidate methods

Six. All classical, all implemented in the accompanying script.

M0 unpreconditioned CG. M1 Jacobi PCG. M2 SSOR PCG, omega = 1.0.
M3 IC(0) PCG. M4 Neumann polynomial preconditioner, degree 4.
M5 Chebyshev polynomial preconditioner, degree 8, bounds from the probe.

Every method solves to the same relative residual tolerance, tau = 1e-8, with the same maximum
iteration budget, 20000. A method that does not reach tau within the budget is recorded as a
failure for that operator and is never silently dropped.

## 3. The probe

k = 30 Lanczos steps on the Jacobi-preconditioned operator, from a seeded random start. It returns
the Ritz values of the k-by-k tridiagonal. Probe wall-clock is measured separately on every
operator and is charged to any method that needs it. Nothing else about the operator is inspected.

## 4. The feature vector, declared

From the probe: log of the Ritz condition estimate, log of the smallest and largest Ritz values,
the three Ritz quartiles, the largest relative gap between consecutive Ritz values, and the mean
and standard deviation of the log Ritz spectrum. From the matrix, at a cost of one pass:
log n, nonzeros per row, and the minimum diagonal dominance ratio. Eleven features. No feature is
added after a result is read.

## 5. The selector

Per method, a gradient-boosted regressor on log end-to-end time, fitted on the training partition
only. The selector takes the argmin of predicted time over the candidate set. Hyperparameters are
fixed here: 200 trees, depth 3, learning rate 0.05, seed 0. They are not tuned.

## 6. The partition

**Leave-one-family-out.** Six folds. In fold i, family i is the test partition and the other five
are training. No operator from a test family appears in training in any form. Within the training
partition a calibration split of 25 per cent, seeded, is reserved for the abstention threshold and
is not used to fit the regressors.

## 7. The score

For operator A, with m-hat the selector's choice and m-o the fastest method measured after the
fact, regret is

    rho(A) = ( T(m-hat, A) - T(m-o, A) ) / T(m-o, A)

the excess end-to-end time as a fraction of the oracle's. T includes probe cost where the method
needs it. We report the median and the 90th percentile of rho over the held-out families, and the
winner accuracy, the fraction of held-out operators where m-hat equals m-o.

## 8. The abstention rule

The selector refuses when the test operator's feature vector lies outside the training support,
measured as a Mahalanobis distance above the 99th percentile of the calibration split's own
distances. On a refusal the system reports *no covering model* and the fallback is the single
method with the best median rank on the training partition. Coverage and the regret on the covered
subset are both reported.

## 9. Registered success criteria

Fixed now, before any result is read.

1. **Primary.** Median regret at most 0.05 and 90th-percentile regret at most 0.20 on held-out
   families, against oracle selection, with all six methods in the candidate set.
2. **Secondary.** Winner accuracy at least 0.80 on held-out families.
3. **Baseline to beat.** A selector using the Ritz condition estimate alone. If the eleven-feature
   selector does not beat it on median regret, we say so.
4. **Null control.** A selector trained on shuffled labels. Its regret is reported beside the real
   one. If the real selector does not separate from it, the study is negative and is reported as
   negative.

**We expect criterion 1 to fail on at least one fold.** The point of leave-one-family-out is that
it can fail, and a failure names which family the features do not carry.

## 10. What will not be run

No comparison against any learned or discovered solver. No distributed or multi-node timing. No
real application matrices from a public collection, because the egress policy on the machine used
blocks those hosts; the operators are generated instead, and that limitation is stated in the paper
rather than worked around.

## 11. Stopping rule

The sweep runs once, over the declared grid. No operator is added, removed or re-run after its
result is read. If a solve fails it stays in the table as a failure.

## Addendum 1, 13 September 2026, before the sweep was run

Written after implementation validation on two of the seventy-two cells and before any cell of the
declared grid was scored. Both items below were under-specified in the text above, and neither
changes the difficulty of the grid.

**A1.1 Convergence is defined by the true residual.** Section 2 said a method solves to a relative
residual of 1e-8 without saying which residual. The recurrence residual carried inside conjugate
gradients drifts from the true residual on ill-conditioned operators. On a smoke-test cell at
conductivity contrast 1e5 the recurrence reported 1e-8 for four methods while the true relative
residual for all four was near 2e-7, so a study that trusted the recurrence would have reported
convergence that did not happen. From here: the true residual is computed every 100 iterations and
at exit, and a run is CONVERGED only if the true relative residual is at or below 1e-8. Runs where
the recurrence claimed convergence and the true residual did not are counted and reported.

**A1.2 The oracle, failures and the wall-clock cap.** The oracle for an operator is the fastest
method that CONVERGED on it. Operators where no method converged carry no selection signal and are
excluded from the regret statistics and reported separately as a count. A method that does not
converge is never treated as if it had. A single solve is additionally capped at 30 seconds of wall
clock, recorded as a timeout, so that one cell cannot consume the budget; the cap is reported
alongside the iteration cap.

**Nothing in the operator grid, the method list, the probe, the feature set, the partition, the
score or the success criteria is changed by this addendum.**

## Addendum 2, 13 September 2026, AFTER the results were read

Recorded post-hoc. Nothing in the operator grid, the method list, the probe, the feature set,
the partition or the registered success criteria of section 9 is changed by this addendum. The
pre-registered score stands exactly as written in section 7 and is reported first everywhere.

**A2.1 A second, post-hoc regret is reported beside the pre-registered one.** Section 7 charges
the probe to a method that needs it, which is M5 alone. That is the right accounting for a
method, and the wrong accounting for a selector: the selector consumes the probe on every
operator in order to choose at all, whatever it then picks. We therefore also report

    rho_deployment(A) = ( T(m-hat, A) + T_probe(A) - T(m-o, A) ) / T(m-o, A)

with no second charge when m-hat is M5, whose T already carries the probe, and no charge against
the oracle, which is clairvoyant and needs no probe. This is a post-hoc addition and is labelled
as one wherever it appears. The pre-registered criteria in section 9 are scored on the
pre-registered regret of section 7 and on nothing else.

**A2.2 A scoring artifact was found stale and has been regenerated.** The `results/scores.json`
committed earlier on 13 September was produced by an earlier revision of `score.py` and did not
reproduce from the recorded `score.py` and `results/raw.jsonl`. Two quantities were affected,
the pooled median regret and the winner accuracy; the ninetieth-percentile regret, the
non-converging counts, the coverage and the abstention figures were identical. The recorded
`score.py` implements the partition this document declares, in which the calibration split is
not used to fit the regressors, so its output is authoritative. `results/scores.json` has been
regenerated from it and the artifact hashes refreshed. The raw sweep was not re-run and
`results/raw.jsonl` is unchanged.

## Addendum 3, 13 September 2026, AFTER the results were read

Recorded post-hoc, on an external reviewer's finding. Nothing in the operator grid, the method
list, the probe, the feature set, the partition or the registered success criteria is changed.

**A3.1 The feature count in section 4 is wrong. There are twelve features, not eleven.** Section 4
enumerates them: from the probe, the log Ritz condition estimate (1), the log smallest and largest
Ritz values (2), the three Ritz quartiles (3), the largest relative gap (1), and the mean and
standard deviation of the log Ritz spectrum (2), which is nine; from the matrix, log n, nonzeros
per row and the minimum diagonal dominance ratio (3). Nine and three is twelve. The sentence
"Eleven features" miscounts its own list. `FEATURE_NAMES` in `probe.py` contains those twelve in
that order and has contained them since before the sweep, so **the feature set was fixed in
advance exactly as enumerated and no feature was added after a result was read**; the enumeration
is authoritative and the word "eleven" is the error. The hash locked at 2026-09-13T15:09:00Z
carries the enumeration and can be checked against it. Every use of "eleven features" in the paper
is corrected to twelve.

**A3.2 Three of the twelve features are exactly linearly dependent, and the consequence was
tested.** The log Ritz condition estimate is by construction the difference of the log largest and
log smallest Ritz values; measured residual over the sixty-five usable operators is 4.4e-16. The
calibration covariance in section 8 is therefore exactly singular, and the ridge of 1e-6 used to
invert it leaves a condition number near 1e8, so a reader is entitled to ask whether the abstention
rule's refusal rate is an artifact of that. It is not. Dropping the dependent feature leaves
pooled coverage unchanged at 0.215; replacing the ridged inverse with a pseudo-inverse lowers
coverage to 0.092, refusing more. The abstention finding is not produced by the singularity. This
is reported in the paper.
